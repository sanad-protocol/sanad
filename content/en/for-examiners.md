<!-- glance -->
- Anyone may attest by publishing a key and signing; verifiers decide whom to trust.
- Attestors are not vetted by the project. Independence rests on declared conflicts, blind marking and the verifier's policy; the protocol does not enforce it.
<!-- /glance -->

An attestation is a signed statement about one piece of evidence: the level you award, your role and whether you declare a conflict of interest.

- Marks are **blind**: you publish a commitment to your mark first and reveal it only after all commitments are in.
- You must declare relationships that could bias you (family, employer, paid tutoring, same cohort). An undeclared conflict that is discovered is recorded as a signed dispute.
- Your sittings and your agreement with other examiners are public and recomputable. Verifiers may require, for example, three sittings and 70 percent agreement.
- Nothing stops a verifier from trusting only a named list of assessors; nothing stops you from attesting for people no verifier knows. That is the decentralised design.
